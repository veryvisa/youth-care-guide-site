This is the brief on discharge and returning to the community. This topic provides a neutral explanation of the rules, time limits, and designated authorities regarding discharge planning and follow-up in British Columbia. Looking directly at the primary legislation. First, the Mental Health Act contains no express duty of discharge planning. If there is no express duty for discharge planning, what are the actual legal requirements?

Well, the Act details a few exact duties. Immediate notice to the near relative after discharge under Section 34.2.2.A, Form 17. A certificate of discharge on application under Section 36.3. And discharge ending the effect of earlier certificates and the like under Section 36.2. Also, the phrase, follow the treatment plan, found in Section 24.2.1, is a consideration for renewal.

It's not a discharge duty. Since the statutory laws lack an express duty for planning, we turn to the ministry's non-legislative guidance. Second, the 2005 guide provides policy recommendations, meaning what should be done, not strict legislation. It suggests a discharge notice may be sent to the near relative before the planned discharge date, and that patients, relatives, and those involved in community care should be included in discharge planning.

I must state plainly that this point is partly verified. Moving to the reality of returning home, who actually takes responsibility once the patient is in the community? Finally, there is absolutely no legal provision that says who is responsible for follow-up after discharge. In practice, as a matter of policy, follow-up is provided by Health Authority Community Mental Health Services and MCFD Child and Youth Mental Health, or CYMH, services.
